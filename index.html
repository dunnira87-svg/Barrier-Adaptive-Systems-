Barrier Adaptive Systems engineers the boundary between a home and its environment. Our systems regulate exterior transportation noise at the envelope so occupants live in a predictable interior field, not one that spikes with every overflight.

The problem

The federal noise threshold and the public health standard are 20 decibels apart.

Federal aircraft noise policy treats a 65 dB day-night average as normally compatible with residential use. The World Health Organization recommends reducing aircraft noise below 45 dB. The two metrics differ, but both are annual averages, and the gap describes a large population living with exposure that a public health body associates with harm and that federal programs do not mitigate.

Survey ranges are 95% confidence ranges. At the federal threshold, the share of residents reporting high annoyance is roughly five times the share on which the threshold was calibrated. The FAA has stated it will not draw policy conclusions from these results until it considers stakeholder input.

The incumbent remedy

Passive insulation attenuates a fixed amount and records nothing.

Federally funded sound insulation has treated thousands of homes near major airports. The public record identifies four structural limits of that model.

Bounded eligibility

Eligibility generally ends at the 65 DNL contour, excluding homes at 50 to 60 DNL where high annoyance is widespread.

Closed windows

Performance depends on windows staying shut, which trades ventilation for quiet.

Static response

Fixed treatments cannot respond to changes in flight paths, operations, or time of night.

Degradation, unmeasured

Installed treatments wear over decades, and none of them measures or reports the exposure it is meant to reduce.

Our approach

Regulate the boundary, not the person.

Individual annoyance tracks discrete events more closely than it tracks annual averages. Our systems are designed around that finding: they target the stability of the interior field and the intrusion of individual events, not maximum suppression.

01 / Measure

Know the exposure

Sensing at the exterior and the interior establishes what arrives at the boundary and what the occupant actually experiences.

02 / Discriminate

Separate noise from signal

Transportation noise is regulated. Alarms, sirens, doorbells, and speech inside the home are never attenuated.

03 / Respond

Act before the event

The system is designed to anticipate predictable exterior events and respond ahead of them, holding the interior near its own baseline.

04 / Record

Show the result

Every zone keeps an exposure record, so performance is measured in the home rather than assumed from a specification.

Market-entry product

A residential boundary system

A residential retrofit system for homes subject to chronic acoustic volatility, beginning with homes beneath aircraft approach and departure corridors and homes adjacent to freeways. It is organized by room function, so a bedroom at night is held to a stricter standard than a kitchen at noon.
